# Fortis rolling five-day PPL plan

This is the active program. It combines barbell strength work, dumbbell presses, and the cables and machines identified at Fortis. The former 57-session binder plan is archived in `fortis-program-legacy.js`; its printed weights are not starting loads for this plan.

## Schedule

Train five days per week, suggested **Monday / Tuesday / Thursday / Friday / Saturday**. Follow **Push A → Pull A → Legs A → Push B → Pull B → Legs B**, continuing across week boundaries.

| Calendar week | Mon | Tue | Thu | Fri | Sat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Push A | Pull A | Legs A | Push B | Pull B |
| 2 | Legs B | Push A | Pull A | Legs A | Push B |
| 3 | Pull B | Legs B | Push A | Pull A | Legs A |

The 12-week schedule contains 60 sessions: ten appearances of every variant. Each push/pull/legs pattern averages 1.67 exposures per week. A particular A or B variant usually recurs every eight or nine days. Missed days move the sequence forward when training resumes; do not cram workouts to meet calendar targets.

Weeks **4, 8 and 12** are lighter calendar weeks. Continue the same sequence; a lighter exposure does not earn a load increase.

Main bench, squat and deadlift sets stop at about **2 RIR**, with **three minutes rest**. Push B bench stays at **2–3 RIR**, with two to three minutes rest. Accessories stay near **2 RIR**; use 90–120 seconds for presses, rows, pulldowns, leg press, belt squat and curls when needed, and about 60–90 seconds for small isolation/core exercises. Allow time for both legs before resting after kneeling curls. Keep pull-ups at two to three minutes if needed to preserve performance.

The two deadlift work sets are the default. A third is unnecessary for completing the prescription and should not displace hamstring curls or main-lift rest. There are no drop sets, plyometrics, explosive Olympic lifts, RDLs or front squats. Put deadlifts on Legs B rather than on Pull immediately before another lower session. Use supported rows to limit unnecessary lower-back fatigue.

## Wenning-inspired warm-up

This is a **scaled adaptation** of Wenning’s variable high-repetition circuits, rather than a full-volume warm-up. His [official sample manual](https://wenningstrength.com/wp-content/uploads/Strength-Training-for-Beginners-SAMPLE.pdf) includes different circuit doses. Every movement is easy, with **at least five clean reps in reserve**. Stop before burning, slowing or straining. Circuit repetitions prepare you to train; they are not extra hard sets or a reason to increase work-set load.

| Session | Circuit before exercise-specific ramps |
| --- | --- |
| Push A / Push B | **Two easy rounds:** cable row 15–20; pressdown 15–20; light cable fly 15 |
| Pull A / Pull B | **Two easy rounds:** light pulldown 15–20; light rear-delt row 15–20; light curl 15 |
| Legs A | **One easy round:** light kneeling curl 12–15 each side; bodyweight squat 10–15; dead bug 5 each side |
| Legs B | **One easy round:** light kneeling curl 12–15 each side; unloaded glute bridge 15; bird dog 5 each side |

Take brief transitions and enough rest to keep this comfortable. Use nearby free stations; if a circuit station is busy, use a light band/dumbbell equivalent rather than reserving multiple machines. On lower days one circuit round accounts for the unilateral curl setup and repetition time. Lighter weeks may use one upper circuit round too.

Follow with progressive, non-fatiguing ramps for the first lift: an empty bar or very light load for 8–10, then a few increasing sets of roughly 5, 3 and 1–2 reps toward the chosen work load. Do not use an untested max to calculate ramps. Deadlift starts from a sensible light floor-height setup. Pull-ups need an easy assisted/bodyweight preparation set; add a lighter preparation set before any later unfamiliar machine or press where useful.

Budget **10–12 minutes total for circuit plus ramps**, including a short easy walk/row if needed. If a circuit makes the first work set weaker, reduce circuit load or volume at the next exposure; do not push through fatigue to satisfy a warm-up quota. The classic full warm-up can consume too much of a 60-minute session.

## Calibration and progression

1. **Choose every starting load manually**, including familiar barbell lifts. Begin at the lower end of the rep range at the prescribed RIR. Warm up and adjust conservatively; the app does not know your starting strength. Do not inherit binder targets or convert dumbbell loads to machine loads.
2. Use the same load while gradually increasing clean reps within the range. When **all prescribed work sets** reach the upper end at the intended RIR, increase by the smallest practical increment at the next normal exposure of that exercise/session variant, and return toward the lower end of the range.
3. If an increment is too large to retain the minimum reps and effort, keep the previous load and accumulate comfortable reps. A stack setting is specific to its machine, seat, grip and range; changing equipment requires recalibration.
4. A missed rep range or effort target means hold or reduce that exercise. Repeat the last successful load/reps; do not advance because a week passed or a checkbox was completed. A and B bench exposures have separate loads and rep ranges.
5. Pull-ups: increase assistance or remove added weight to meet the range. Reduce assistance before adding external weight. For unilateral work, use the same setup/load, start with the harder side, and match its clean reps with the other side; do not add extra stronger-side reps.
6. Ab wheel progresses controlled reps first, then reach only when trunk position remains steady. Pallof press uses the listed reps per side; raise resistance only after all sets remain controlled at the intended effort.

Actual performed loads/reps/RIR are the evidence for progression; session targets are a separate editable plan. No automatic loading is inferred from dates, completed sessions or a lighter week's easier work.

## Lighter weeks and recovery

During calendar weeks **4, 8 and 12**, keep rolling through the session variants. Use **two sets for each main barbell lift**, including deadlift, and **one set for every accessory**. Use the lower end of the rep range at **4+ RIR**, usually with less load; select the load from actual recent performance rather than a fixed percentage or an old printed target. Use one easy circuit round and appropriate ramps. No progress is earned from these deliberately easier sessions.

Return to the last successful normal-exposure load after a lighter week, reducing further if needed. If performance repeatedly drops across several lifts with poor recovery, bring a lighter period forward. A single stalled exercise normally calls for a local adjustment. Stop a movement that causes new pain and choose an appropriate alternative rather than treating this written plan as an injury diagnosis.

## The 60-minute cap

The cap includes circuits, ramps, working sets, all rests and equipment transitions. Normal sessions contain **11 sets on Push A / Pull A / Pull B**, **12 on Push B**, and **13 on either Legs day**. A unilateral set means both sides and takes longer than a bilateral set.

A workable budget is 10–12 minutes for preparation, 12–16 for the first lift, and the remaining 32–38 for accessories and transitions. These are planning estimates, not guaranteed completion times in a busy gym. Lower-day unilateral curls make those sessions the tightest.

- Preserve main-lift rest and hamstring curls. On Legs A, trim the final leg-press or calf set if behind; on Legs B, trim the final belt-squat or calf set.
- Pair only nearby free isolation/core stations. Never rely on occupying two popular machines at once to meet the schedule.
- Do not add mandatory sleds, carries, finishers or extra exercises on top of this plan. Keep the sequence sustainable before adding optional work.

## Muscle coverage and imbalance review

The previous four-core-day plan already trained the major muscle groups, but dedicated rear-delt work depended on optional E and horizontal rowing had only four weekly sets. The revised plan puts rear delts in both mandatory pull variants and retains rows alongside vertical pulling. It keeps direct knee flexion on both lower variants rather than counting deadlifts as a substitute for curls.

| Emphasis | Sets per complete six-session rotation | Average per five-session week |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chest: bench, incline press, fly | 11 | 9.2 |
| Vertical pulls | 6 | 5 |
| Horizontal rows | 6 | 5 |
| Dedicated rear delts | 4 | 3.3 |
| Lateral delts | 6 | 5 |
| Shoulder press | 2 | 1.7 |
| Direct biceps | 6 | 5 |
| Direct triceps | 4 | 3.3 |
| Quad-dominant squat, leg press, belt squat | 8 | 6.7 |
| Knee-flexion hamstring curls | 6 | 5 |
| Conventional deadlift | 2 | 1.7 |
| Calves | 6 | 5 |
| Direct core | 4 | 3.3 |

These describe **normal loading sessions**, before lighter-week reductions or time-cap cuts. The weekly average multiplies rotation sets by five-sixths; any individual calendar week will differ. Unilateral sets are counted once per trained side, not doubled into a false muscle-volume total. Muscle contributions overlap: pressing also trains triceps/anterior delts; pulling trains biceps; squats, belt squats and deadlifts load glutes; deadlifts also load spinal erectors. These counts do not assign every overlapping set full credit to every muscle.

Glutes and spinal erectors have meaningful compound exposure, so no extra hinge is added. Dedicated adductor/abductor, neck, forearm and shrug work is not included by default; this does not establish a deficiency. Grip works during pulls and deadlifts. Front-deltoid isolation is unnecessary given pressing volume. No muscle group depends on an optional sixth session.

A written program can show differences in **training emphasis**, but cannot establish left/right weakness, posture, injury risk or physique imbalance. Compare equal-load, equal-rep, equal-effort unilateral performances over several sessions before concluding there is a persistent difference. The stated priority is balanced strength and muscle growth with manageable fatigue, rather than a promise that equal set counts produce equal muscle growth.

## Equipment and evidence

Equipment choices use the [Fortis equipment list](https://fortisfitness.ca/our-equipment/) and [Fortis East location](https://fortisfitness.ca/locations/fortis-fitness-east/): supported rows, leg press, Atlantis kneeling curl and Wenning belt squat are the intended stations. Incline and shoulder presses remain dumbbell movements; standing calves remain dumbbell work. No seated leg-curl, incline/shoulder-press machine or standing-calf machine is assumed. Check the actual station's setup before calibrating. Use the rear-delt machine if present, otherwise the listed cable reverse fly.

Machines and free weights can both produce strength and muscle growth. An eight-week study of 38 resistance-trained men found similar assessed muscle growth and overall strength improvements, with the strongest strength improvements in the trained modality. This supports retaining barbell strength practice while choosing useful machine/cable accessories; it does not prove every machine is safer or every substitution equivalent. [Hernández-Belmonte et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37535335/).

The circuits above are our time-limited adaptation of the Wenning approach, not a claim that this exact warm-up has demonstrated superior injury prevention or hypertrophy.
